In this work, we presented DISCO-TAB, a unified framework for biomedical tabular data synthesis that bridges the gap between the interpretability of rule-based systems and the generative power of LLMs. By orchestrating a fine-tuned GPT model with a hierarchical, multi-objective discriminator system, we demonstrated that it is possible to synthesize clinical records that are not only statistically faithful but also structurally and semantically consistent.

Our evaluation across 23 benchmarks and 21 metrics indicates that DISCO-TAB attains top-tier downstream utility among generators with full benchmark coverage, leads that field on correlation preservation, and is among the most reliable methods we study, consistently avoiding the single-class collapse seen in the small-sample, severely imbalanced regimes common to medical research. Inverse-Frequency Reward Shaping is designed to keep rare pathological cases from being ignored, while Automated Constraint Discovery preserves the pairwise clinical dependencies important for decision support.

As healthcare moves towards data-centric AI, DISCO-TAB offers a \rev{practical} mechanism to \rev{help} unlock the value of siloed EHR repositories. \rev{Future work will focus on three concrete directions: (i) integrating a formal differential-privacy mechanism (e.g., DP-SGD or a PATE-style aggregation) into the RL reward loop to provide bounded privacy guarantees; (ii) extending Automated Constraint Discovery beyond pairwise statistics toward higher-order and causal dependencies, ideally with clinician-in-the-loop validation of clinical plausibility; and (iii) validating the framework on large, multi-institutional EHR cohorts and extending the hierarchical discriminator to longitudinal, multi-modal patient trajectories.}
