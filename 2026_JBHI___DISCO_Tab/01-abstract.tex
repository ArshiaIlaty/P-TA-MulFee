The development of robust clinical decision support systems is frequently impeded by the scarcity of high-fidelity biomedical data that can be shared with limited disclosure risk. Generative large language models (LLMs) are a promising route to synthetic data, yet they struggle to capture the non-linear dependencies and severe class imbalance of electronic health records (EHRs), often producing records that are statistically plausible but structurally or semantically inconsistent. We introduce DISCO-TAB (DIScriminator-guided COntrol for TABular synthesis), which couples a fine-tuned LLM generator with a reinforcement-learning objective shaped by a hierarchy of discriminators. Instead of a single scalar signal, the generator receives feedback at four granularities, token, sentence, feature, and row, combined with automated constraint discovery and inverse-frequency reward shaping that protects minority classes. Across a 23-dataset benchmark spanning clinical, financial, chemical, and web domains and 21 metrics, DISCO-TAB attains top-tier downstream utility among generators with full benchmark coverage and leads that field on correlation preservation, and it consistently avoids the single-class collapse that competing diffusion and language-model generators exhibit on the smallest clinical datasets. It maintains a calibrated disclosure-risk profile, keeping the distance to the closest real record near the real--real baseline rather than inflating it with noise, so disclosure risk is characterized empirically rather than through a formal privacy guarantee. These results position DISCO-TAB as a dependable option for utility-preserving tabular synthesis in the data-scarce, imbalanced clinical settings where existing generators are most fragile.
